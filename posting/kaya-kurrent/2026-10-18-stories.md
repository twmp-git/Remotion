> 🇲🇼 Mzuzu Central Hospital plans to buy new equipment for its operating theatres, wards and maternity unit.
> 
> A tender notice dated 8 October asks suppliers to quote for seven lots. The biggest items are two anaesthesia machines, one ultrasound machine and four LED theatre lights.
> 
> The anaesthesia machines keep patients asleep and breathing safely during surgery. The theatre lights let surgeons see clearly while they operate.
> 
> The list also includes 10 infusion pumps, which control drips, and 8 diathermy machines, which surgeons use to cut tissue and stop bleeding. There are 6 vein finders, which help nurses find a vein for a drip or an injection.
> 
> For mothers, the hospital wants a CTG machine. It tracks an unborn baby's heartbeat during labour.
> 
> Mzuzu Central Hospital is the main referral hospital for the Northern Region. Patients from Karonga, Chitipa, Rumphi, Nkhata Bay, Likoma and Mzimba are sent there for care their district hospitals cannot give.
> 
> Suppliers must hand in sealed quotes by 10am on Tuesday, 27 October. Quotes go in the tender box in the hospital library, in the Administration Block. They must be valid for 30 days, and the goods must arrive within 30 days of an order.
> 
> The notice does not say how the equipment will be paid for, or what it will replace.

> 🇲🇼 Mangochi District Council plans to buy 166 goats for families under Traditional Authority Lulanga.
> 
> A notice dated 14 October asks suppliers to quote for 160 female goats and 6 Boer bucks. Boer goats are a breed raised for meat, and the bucks will be used for breeding.
> 
> The council has set rules for the goats. Each female must be at least nine months old, come from a family that has had twins, have a good record of feeding its young, and be healthy. Each buck must be at least one year old, with strong legs and good muscle.
> 
> All the goats must come from the same area of Mangochi, so they are used to local conditions.
> 
> The notice's reference number includes 'MBSPIII'. Copies of quotes must be emailed to the council, with a copy to an Icelandic government email address.
> 
> Suppliers must deliver the goats to the council within 45 days of an order. Quotes close at 2pm on 19 October. Sealed quotes go to the Chairperson of the council's Internal Procurement and Disposal Committee, Private Bag 138, Mangochi.
> 
> The notice does not say how many families will receive goats or how they were chosen.

> 🇲🇼 An Australian company says its rare earths mine in Balaka District is on track to start producing before the end of 2026.
> 
> Lindian Resources owns the Kangankunde project. Rare earths are a group of minerals used in phones, electric car motors and wind turbines. Demand for them has grown fast around the world.
> 
> The company fired its first mining blast in July. That blast broke about 13,100 tonnes of rock, including an estimated 5,500 tonnes of ore. Lorries have since been moving the ore to stockpiles at the site.
> 
> By late June, the company said it had built a 27-kilometre power line to the site. It had also reached the halfway point on a dam for mine waste, called a tailings storage facility.
> 
> In July, the company said it was targeting October to start up the processing plant and mid-November to finish building it. It aims to produce its first rare earths in the October to December quarter.
> 
> A study due in December will look at expanding the mine to 120,000 tonnes of concentrate a year. Any expansion depends on that study and a final decision by the company.
> 
> Executive director Zac Komur called the first blast 'a defining execution milestone'. He said the company went from building to mining in under 12 months.
